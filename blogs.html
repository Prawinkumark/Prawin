Movies: two industries, one love of stories

I watch films from both sides of the world. Here is what I enjoy most about Indian cinema and Hollywood, and why I think each one is beautiful in its own way.

Indian cinema Hollywood Best of both
Indian cinema

Films that make you laugh, cry and sing in one sitting

Indian cinema is not one industry. It is many: Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Kannada, Hindi, Bengali, Marathi and more. Each has its own language, its own heroes and its own taste in stories. Together they make more films every year than almost any country on earth.

Emotion is the main ingredient

An Indian film is rarely just one genre. A single movie can move from comedy to romance to a fight scene to a family drama, and it somehow works. Audiences are happy to feel everything. That is why the best of these films stay with you long after the lights come on.

Music and dance carry the story

Songs are not a break from the plot. They are the plot: a love blossoming, a hero finding courage, a village celebrating. Composers like A. R. Rahman and Ilaiyaraaja turned film music into something the whole country hums, from tea stalls to wedding halls.

Culture you can see and hear

Festivals, family dinners, temple streets, monsoon rain, crowded trains. Indian films are full of the details of everyday life, which makes even a big-budget epic feel personal. And in the last few years, regional cinema has proved that a story told in one language can win hearts everywhere.

A great Indian film does not ask you to pick one feeling. It gives you all of them.
  • Nayakan (1987)A Tamil crime drama with Kamal Haasan that is still taught as a masterclass in acting and direction.
  • Lagaan (2001)A village cricket match against colonial rule, told with heart, music and real tension.
  • Baahubali (2015, 2017)Proof that Indian cinema can build a mythic world on the same scale as any epic.
  • Premam (2015)A gentle Malayalam coming-of-age story that many viewers watched again and again.
  • RRR (2022)A Telugu action spectacle that made audiences around the world cheer in theatres.
Hollywood

The art of spectacle and the craft behind it

Hollywood has been shaping how the world watches films for more than a century. Whatever you think of blockbusters, it is hard to deny the sheer craft: sound design that you feel in your chest, visual effects that make the impossible look real, and editing that keeps you gripped for two hours.

Big ideas, tightly told

Hollywood is brilliant at the high-concept pitch. A dream inside a dream. A toy that comes alive. A ship that meets an iceberg. The best of these films take a simple, bold idea and build every scene around it, so the story feels sharp and complete.

Technology as storytelling

From the first Star Wars to Avatar, Hollywood keeps pushing what a camera and a computer can do. Practical effects, motion capture and IMAX photography are not just tricks. They let filmmakers show worlds that no one had seen before.

Directors with a voice

Alongside the franchises, there are directors who treat cinema like a personal art form. Christopher Nolan plays with time, Steven Spielberg with wonder, Quentin Tarantino with dialogue and style, and Martin Scorsese with character. Watching their films, you can often tell who made them within minutes.

Hollywood at its best makes the impossible feel ordinary, and the ordinary feel unforgettable.
  • The Shawshank Redemption (1994)A quiet story about hope that grew into one of the most loved films ever made.
  • The Dark Knight (2008)A superhero film that works as a serious crime thriller, anchored by a legendary villain.
  • Inception (2010)Layers of dreams, a ticking clock and a score that you will not forget.
  • Interstellar (2014)Space travel with a father and daughter at its centre.
  • Parasite (2019)Technically a Korean film, but its Best Picture win changed what Hollywood's biggest night celebrates.
Best of both

What each side can teach the other

I do not think of this as a contest. Indian cinema reminds Hollywood that emotion and music can be bold and unashamed. Hollywood reminds Indian cinema that a tight script and polished craft make a story hit harder.

And they are already learning from each other. Indian films now use world-class effects and sound. Hollywood keeps borrowing from global stories, and audiences everywhere are watching subtitled films without a second thought.

What I love most is that a movie can make a stranger on the other side of the world feel what you feel. That is the real beauty of cinema, whatever language it speaks.
